# The Right It: hypotheses and pretotyping methods

- Turn an idea into a measurable claim about **who will do what**, then design the smallest practical experiment that actually observes that behavior.
- Savoia supplies eight principal pretotyping techniques, plus combinations and variations. The artifact alone is insufficient: it must be used to collect firsthand evidence with a meaningful commitment.
- Interest, payment, repeat use, feasibility, and business viability remain different claims. A convincing demo or a successful local test does not settle them all.

## Source-stated method inventory

`RIGHTIT-*` identifiers are our reference labels, not the author's worksheet IDs. These are concise paraphrases of source procedures; **Limits** state this research note's interpretation unless explicitly attributed. They do not create a mandatory sequence of expert sessions.

|---|---|---|
| `RIGHTIT-YODA` | A market claim and candidate evidence → check freshness, direct relevance, known collection provenance, and sufficient support; collect firsthand data for this idea → **Your Own DAta**, or YODA. | Chapter 3 permits others' data to inform and supplement decisions, while rejecting it as a substitute. The glossary additionally requires **skin in the game**, meaning something of value committed by the audience. Firsthand collection alone is insufficient. [Ch. 3, Data Beats Opinions and YODA](derived/the-right-it/011-chapter-3.md#_idParaDest-9); [Glossary](derived/the-right-it/021-glossary.md#page_252). |
| `RIGHTIT-MEH` | Product premise and intended audience → state how people are expected to engage: investigate, try, adopt, buy, repeat, or recommend → **Market Engagement Hypothesis**. | A concise initial belief, not a result. Distinguish the behaviors rather than treating generic enthusiasm as all of them. [Ch. 4, Market Engagement Hypothesis](derived/the-right-it/013-chapter-4.md#_idParaDest-13a). |
| `RIGHTIT-XYZ` | Vague MEH → express “At least X% of Y will Z,” replacing ambiguous audience, amount, behavior, price, and conditions with explicit values → a testable hypothesis. | Initial numbers may be educated guesses, informed by minimum viable market needs; unnecessary decimal precision is discouraged. Agreement on numbers exposes hidden team disagreements but does not establish demand. [Ch. 4, Say It with Numbers / XYZ Hypothesis](derived/the-right-it/013-chapter-4.md#_idParaDest-13b). |

Changing purchase into a lighter behavior creates another assumption to test; the broader claim is not proved automatically. [Ch. 4, Hypozooming](derived/the-right-it/013-chapter-4.md#_idParaDest-13c); [Glossary definition](derived/the-right-it/021-glossary.md#page_250). |
| `RIGHTIT-PRETOTYPE-CONTRACT` | An uncertainty about whether a product merits investment → choose or combine techniques that produce YODA with commitment, quickly and cheaply → a market experiment before disproportionate construction. | Savoia distinguishes this aim from prototypes primarily investigating construction or operation. Labels do not establish evidence quality, and feasibility can still be an independent uncertainty. [Ch. 5, Pretotyping](derived/the-right-it/014-chapter-5.md#_idParaDest-15b); [three requirements](derived/the-right-it/014-chapter-5.md#_idParaDest-15m). |
| `RIGHTIT-MECHANICAL-TURK` | Expensive or unavailable automation with a function people can perform → simulate the system using a human operator → observations of actual engagement with the offered experience, potentially including payment. | Tests the experience/demand under simulated delivery, not whether automation will work, scale, or have acceptable costs. Fold4U's repeated tests explicitly examine declining novelty. [Ch. 5, Mechanical Turk](derived/the-right-it/014-chapter-5.md#_idParaDest-15d); [repeat testing](derived/the-right-it/014-chapter-5.md#page_94). |

The PalmPilot account explicitly says one inventor's use cannot validate everyone else's demand. [Ch. 5, Pinocchio](derived/the-right-it/014-chapter-5.md#_idParaDest-15e); [sample limitation](derived/the-right-it/014-chapter-5.md#page_97). |

[Ch. 5, YouTube](derived/the-right-it/014-chapter-5.md#_idParaDest-15h); [continued engagement](derived/the-right-it/014-chapter-5.md#page_124). |

[Ch. 5, One-Night Stand](derived/the-right-it/014-chapter-5.md#_idParaDest-15i). |

Borrowed traffic and credibility remain part of the tested context. | `RIGHTIT-MORSEL` | A concept whose appeal depends on experiencing its content → release a small usable sample, then invite a commitment for more → evidence of consumption, return, contribution, or purchase. | A sample must preserve the appeal being tested. The serial-fiction example illustrates repeated engagement; it is not a universal conversion benchmark for software. [Ch. 5, Morsel](derived/the-right-it/014-chapter-5.md#page_145). |

## Evidence boundaries to preserve

**Record the observed event exactly.** Savoia's [squirrel-book example](derived/the-right-it/014-chapter-5.md#page_113) calls advertising spend divided by unfulfilled “Buy” clicks customer acquisition cost and extrapolates sales. For our evidence records that observation is **cost per buy-intent click**; completed customer acquisition and revenue remain unobserved. Likewise, [the joke-book example](derived/the-right-it/014-chapter-5.md#page_141) translates a 5% purchase hypothesis into a 25% pickup hypothesis and assumes a later one-in-five conversion is plausible. Preserve that untested bridge; do not mark purchases validated by pickups. The [FeeBird illustration](derived/the-right-it/images/007-image127.jpg) shows a simulated $5 unlock from approximate to precise location, not a payment transaction.

**Numerical specificity is not statistical certainty.** The [100–1,000-person suggestion](derived/the-right-it/013-chapter-4.md#page_75) is Savoia's general heuristic, not a significance guarantee or a prescribed sample for every idea. Precision, selection, independent observations, baseline rates, and the decision's stakes still matter. His example thresholds, budgets, and time limits demonstrate a process, not transferable acceptance rules. The broad claim that a true XYZ must imply a particular xyz depends on the audience and behavior mapping; a selected local niche can differ from the whole market.

**Do not turn a methodological preference into a prohibition on research.** Chapter 3 [permits Other People's Data to inform decisions](derived/the-right-it/011-chapter-3.md#_idParaDest-9a), whereas the [Glossary](derived/the-right-it/021-glossary.md#page_252) discourages seeking it and ultimately calls YODA necessary and sufficient. Retain the narrower operational conclusion: external research can expose possibilities and confounders, while firsthand behavioral evidence is needed for claims about this idea. Neither the author's nor an agent's expertise is a customer's action. Interviews about concrete past behavior can inform experiment design; hypothetical approval must not become purchase evidence.

**Match the experiment to the claim.** A manually fulfilled service can reveal demand without verifying its future automation. The author's [FeeBird discussion](derived/the-right-it/014-chapter-5.md#page_126) assumes negligible construction uncertainty for that illustration; this is not a rule that all software is technically straightforward. Similarly, founder role-play can meaningfully inform a personal tool while remaining insufficient for a broad market. The [book overview](derived/the-right-it/007-book-overview.md#page_6) already includes features, services, nonprofits, and nonmonetary engagement within its scope.

## Proposed mentor-room application — our synthesis

These are suggested facilitation prompts and records, not a Savoia-authored worksheet or a settled skill contract:

1. **Clarify the next investment.** Ask what “worth pursuing” means for this idea and which uncertainty could change the next decision. Separate the whole configuration from any independently testable component.
2. **Reuse what exists.** Inspect earlier work against the audience, offer, behavior, context, recency, collection method, and evidence requirements. Credit valid work and name the actual gap before prescribing another exercise.
3. **Make the claim observable.** Ask: who qualifies for the denominator; what precise action counts; at what price or effort; within what period; and why would this result justify the next investment? Write the MEH, XYZ, reachable xyz, and any untested bridge between them.
4. **Choose a proportionate experiment.** Compare two or three relevant techniques, identify what each can answer, then select one. Record participant expectations and how the promise will be delivered or clarified. For software, a manually operated workflow, a tiny usable feature, or a video plus explicit commitment may be sufficient.
5. **Keep results scoped.** Save raw counts and observations separately from interpretations, commitments separately from fulfillment, and first use separately from return. Revise a hypothesis with a new version instead of erasing its prior failure. Preserve promising components with their original evidence context; a new audience or configuration requires a new applicability check.

The session's concrete deliverable is an experiment brief and an evidence record, followed by a justified next action—not an expert vote. A personal-tool adaptation may specify observed use and time saved for its single intended user, without forcing a mass-market percentage. Commercial adaptations may require later evidence on actual payment, retention, distribution, and delivery economics. These scope choices are ours, grounded in the user's goals.

Savoia's methods supplement the mandatory Knapp–Zeratsky work. Shared artifacts may satisfy overlapping needs after explicit checking; selecting a pretotype does not silently waive required Foundation Sprint or Design Sprint work. Keep each idea case's history independent, and treat any transfer of component evidence as a stated hypothesis to examine. This research note is ready for future skill design; it is not itself a tested mentor-room skill.
