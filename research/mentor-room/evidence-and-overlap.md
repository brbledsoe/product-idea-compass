# Evidence methods that can share work without sharing conclusions

- Interviews clarify experienced needs; behavioral experiments test specific actions; usability tests assess interaction; PMF diagnostics require actual product experience. These are complementary evidence streams.
- Reuse the underlying observation when the customer, claim, context, and quality match. Do not count multiple mentor interpretations as independent evidence.
- Route by the user's intended outcome: a useful feature, a sustainable small business, and a venture-scale company have different success criteria.

Accessed **2026-09-05**. This note verifies selected core methods from `resources.csv`, rather than accepting its descriptions. The matrix summarizes public primary sources; the subsequent operating rules are **proposed synthesis for the future skill**, not claims that these authors prescribe one combined process. ## Verified methods serve different decisions

| Method | Purpose and inputs | Output and evidence | Scope and limitations |
|---|---|---|---|
| Alberto Savoia: pretotyping | Identify an assumption whose failure defeats the idea; specify a target group and measurable action. | A minimally simulated experience, a market-engagement hypothesis, and observed interaction with it. | Tests appeal and usage cheaply, initially in a narrow real-world setting. The official methodology contrasts this with prototypes focused on construction/performance questions. Its definition of “prototype” should not be silently imposed on other authors. [Official methodology](https://www.pretotyping.org/methodology.html) |
| Rob Fitzpatrick / Eric Migicovsky: customer conversations | Learn about a person's actual circumstances, recent problems, and attempted remedies; avoid pitching and hypothetical purchase questions. | Contextual accounts of past behavior, existing alternatives, and their shortcomings. Fitzpatrick also identifies commitment and advancement as ways to assess early interest. | Suitable before and after a product exists. Interview technique improves the usefulness of reported evidence; a good conversation alone does not demonstrate future purchase. [YC's first-party transcript](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/startup-school-week-1-recap-kevin-hale-and-eric-migicovsky/), [Fitzpatrick's teacher guidance](https://www.momtestbook.com/teachers) |
| David Bland / Strategyzer: assumption mapping | Surface business hypotheses and prioritize their importance against supporting evidence. | A map identifying important, poorly supported assumptions for testing. | Covers desirability, feasibility, and viability; the current public article also names adaptability. Preserve that source/version distinction when comparing taxonomies. [Bland's explanation](https://www.strategyzer.com/library/how-assumptions-mapping-can-focus-your-teams-on-running-experiments-that-matter) |
| Strategyzer: Test and Learning Cards | An explicit hypothesis, test, metric, and success threshold before running an experiment. | Observations, a distinct interpretation of them, and the resulting action. | These are structures for designing and learning from experiments, not evidence by themselves. Many tests can be required. [Test Card](https://www.strategyzer.com/library/validate-your-ideas-with-the-test-card), [Learning Card](https://www.strategyzer.com/library/capture-customer-insights-and-actions-with-the-learning-card) |
| Teresa Torres: Opportunity Solution Tree | A target-customer/value-proposition theory, a defined outcome, and **three to four story-based customer interviews** are stated prerequisites. | A connected view of outcome, customer opportunities, alternative solutions, and assumption tests. | Opportunities are customer needs, pains, or desires, not invented feature requests. Torres generally favors a product outcome rather than adoption of one predetermined feature. [Torres's guide](https://www.producttalk.org/opportunity-solution-trees/) |
| Marty Cagan: four risks | Examine a proposed solution for value, usability, feasibility, and business viability. | A differentiated discovery agenda covering whether people choose it, can use it, whether the team can deliver it, and whether it works for the business. | The article provides risk categories and responsibilities, not a universal pass threshold. Viability includes channels, economics, and organizational constraints. [Cagan's article](https://www.svpg.com/four-big-risks/) |
| Rob Walling: 2/20/200 | Escalate effort while evaluating ideas. | Approximate **hours**: an initial 5 P.M. screen, then landing-page/customer-conversation evidence, then a usable MVP that can be manual or no-code. | Written for bootstrapped or mostly bootstrapped founders; channel choice influences the middle stage. These are approximate investment bands, not required participant counts or calibrated probabilities of success. [Episode 706 transcript, especially 10:27 onward](https://www.startupsfortherestofus.com/episodes/episode-706-2-20-200-validation-prior-art-and-designing-by-committee-a-rob-solo-adventure) |
| Rahul Vohra: Superhuman PMF engine | Survey people who recently experienced the product's core value; analyze relevant segments. | A disappointment measure, segment insights, and roadmap direction. | Vohra describes using people active at least twice in two weeks and a 40% “very disappointed” benchmark. Treat this as a reported diagnostic practice, not a universal proof of PMF, profitability, or raw-idea demand. [Vohra's first-person account](https://review.firstround.com/how-superhuman-built-an-engine-to-find-product-market-fit/) |

## Proposed reuse rules retain the question each method answers

1. **One observation record, several uses.** A dated interview account can inform customer understanding, an opportunity, a positioning hypothesis, and a risk. Keep the participant/context reference, reported behavior, interpretation, and unanswered questions separate. Existing work earns credit only after this audit; a persona invented by an agent remains a hypothesis.
2. **One experiment can satisfy several method formats.** A Savoia-style behavioral hypothesis can fit a Strategyzer Test Card and address a Cagan value risk. Preserve its actual metric: an email signup measures that action, not payment or retention. This proposed mapping follows the different purposes in the matrix, rather than claiming formal equivalence among frameworks.
3. **Do not flatten evidence into a universal ladder.** Strategyzer distinguishes fast exploratory interviews/surveys from stronger purchasing evidence near a real buying situation. That comparison is about the purchasing claim; interviews can still be the appropriate evidence for understanding a past struggle. Select evidence for the question being answered. [Strategyzer on evidence strength](https://www.strategyzer.com/library/how-strong-is-your-innovation-evidence)
4. **Keep a usability result local.** Successfully completing a prototype task can reduce usability uncertainty while value, feasibility, and viability remain open. This is an inference from Cagan's separate risk definitions, not an additional Cagan rule. [Four risks](https://www.svpg.com/four-big-risks/)
5. **Do not collapse solution exploration into one yes/no test.** A tree can compare alternatives for an evidenced opportunity; an individual pretotype probes an assumption. The proposed room should retain alternatives and negative findings rather than treating one failed implementation as disproof of the underlying need. [Torres](https://www.producttalk.org/opportunity-solution-trees/), [Pretotyping](https://www.pretotyping.org/methodology.html)
6. **Keep each idea's history separate and evidence scoped.** Within one idea, distinguish the whole proposition from components and their tested contexts. A failed combination can preserve a promising component; that component still needs evidence for any standalone proposition or new context.

## Proposed routing separates feature value from company ambition

| User's intended result | Proposed evaluation emphasis | Avoid importing automatically |
|---|---|---|
| Feature in existing software | A customer/product outcome, alternatives, incremental value, usability, integration constraints, and actual effects after release. | Venture-scale market requirements or treating whole-product PMF as validation of the feature. |
| Bootstrapped product | Reachable buyers, sustainable delivery/acquisition economics, founder capacity, and progressively stronger commitments. | Rejecting a useful niche solely because its ceiling is too low for venture returns. |
| Venture-scale company | Demand and execution evidence plus a credible path to sufficiently rapid and large growth. | Treating an attractive market or investment narrative as customer evidence. |

This routing is synthesis anchored in a real source difference: Kevin Hale explicitly frames YC evaluation around fast growth and venture interest; Walling explicitly frames his episode around bootstrapped or mostly bootstrapped founders. Their screens should inform different ambitions rather than become contradictory universal gates. [Hale's transcript](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/startup-school-week-1-recap-kevin-hale-and-eric-migicovsky/), [Walling's transcript](https://www.startupsfortherestofus.com/episodes/episode-706-2-20-200-validation-prior-art-and-designing-by-committee-a-rob-solo-adventure)

## Public coverage supports a pilot, with explicit gaps

All cited pages were readable. The CSV's [Bland webinar page](https://www.strategyzer.com/library/stratchat-webinar-replay-testing-business-ideas-with-david-bland) confirms the talk but supplies no substantive transcript; the method details above come from the linked authors' public articles. Fitzpatrick's teacher page identifies topics, not the book's complete exercises. No full book, paid course, or complete experiment library was obtained; the future skill must not label these methods exhaustively implemented on this basis. Knapp–Zeratsky source coverage and remaining CSV mentors are outside this note's assignment.

The pilot should end with a scoped decision such as “fund the next experiment,” “pursue under these conditions,” “pause,” or “stop this version,” plus unresolved risks and evidence that would change the decision. Mentor agreement and completed worksheets are process progress; they do not substitute for observations from real customers.
