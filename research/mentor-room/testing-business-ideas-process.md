# Testing Business Ideas: process, decisions, and operating conditions

- Break an idea into explicit hypotheses, prioritize important uncertainties, and connect each experiment to evidence, learning, and a decision.
- The Assumptions Map, Test Card, Learning Card, and experiment board provide useful continuity between mentor conversations and real-world work.
- This is a repeated learning process. Neither completed templates nor the book's qualitative confidence labels constitute a universal validation score.

## Decision and evidence limits

The four [confidence levels](derived/testing-business-ideas/page-073.md) are qualitative: **Not Confident at All** follows one weak experiment; **Not Really Confident** reflects interviews/surveys about future intentions; **Somewhat Confident** follows several strong experiments or a particularly strong call-to-action test; **Very Confident** requires several experiments including at least one call-to-action test producing very strong evidence. These are the authors' practice guidelines, not statistical confidence intervals or a calibrated chance of business success.

Likewise, sample counts, accuracy targets, and satisfaction thresholds in the [worked cards](derived/testing-business-ideas/page-062.md) illustrate specific experiments. They are not universal acceptance gates. The [evidence-strength examples](derived/testing-business-ideas/page-069.md) visually rate interviews one of five circles, search trends three, and concierge five; these compare the illustrated observations, not every use of each technique. For our records, retain what actually occurred and whether it supports this claim; do not convert pictograms into a validation percentage.

The [decision flow](derived/testing-business-ideas/page-079.md) keeps unclear insights distinct from refutation. Supporting evidence means further testing, potentially at higher fidelity, rather than automatic permission to scale. The [pivot definition](derived/testing-business-ideas/page-077.md) explicitly warns that previously collected evidence may no longer apply. The book also warns against incomparable contexts, weak data, confirmation bias, too few experiments, and failure to act on learning. Its critique of outsourcing concerns losing the rapid learning/adaptation loop, not a ban on expert assistance ([PDF 331](derived/testing-business-ideas/page-331.md)).

Organizational prescriptions need scope checks. The ceremony overview lists fixed times, while detailed agendas allow ranges: weekly planning/learning 30-60 minutes, retrospective 30-60, stakeholder review 60-90. These are working cadences, not required durations for a chat session. The [portfolio table](derived/testing-business-ideas/page-342.md) supplies funding/team-size ranges and experiment-theme proportions for Seed, Launch, Growth; none is a scoring scheme for personal tools. The [committee section](derived/testing-business-ideas/page-343.md) distinguishes monthly reviews from typical 3-6-month investment decisions.

## Proposed mentor-room use, separate from source prescriptions

This book supplies enough concrete process material to begin designing the room with the current source library. Further uploads are not a prerequisite. This is a readiness observation, not a finalized architecture or a claim that the resulting skill has been tested.

For a case, retain one connected chain: idea/component, hypothesis, reason it matters, prior evidence, experiment, raw result, interpretation, next decision. Existing work can be credited after checking audience, context, recency, method, and acceptance criteria; this matches the source's demand to challenge evidence already on the map. Keep promising components when a configuration fails, with their original context attached. A new market or combination triggers an applicability review, not automatic evidence inheritance.

Adapt scope to the user's definition of worthwhile. Commercial ideas need revenue/cost questions; a personal tool can instead test useful behavior, effort, and sustainable maintenance. That substitution is our adaptation, not the book's financial definition of viability. Team ceremonies and portfolio committees are relevant only when those coordination needs exist; a single user should still receive a clear next action and a place to resume.

The [Right It methods](the-right-it-methods.md) can sharpen a Test Card's claim and observable commitment. [Pincus's methods](pincus-idea-methods.md) help isolate an atomic idea and distinguish initial interest from durable use. Those connections are synthesis, not author-endorsed equivalence. Knapp-Zeratsky coverage remains mandatory under the user's request: shared artifacts may satisfy overlapping work after checking the actual requirements, but this process does not silently replace the Foundation Sprint or Design Sprint. Mentor expertise guides questions and experiments; it is not customer evidence.
