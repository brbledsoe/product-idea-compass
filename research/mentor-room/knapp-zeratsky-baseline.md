## Foundation Sprint establishes a hypothesis to test

The following activity inventory summarizes the authors’ [Foundation Sprint guide](https://www.character.vc/guide/foundation-sprint), locators **Set the Stage**, **Day 1**, **Day 2**, and **After the Foundation Sprint**. Text was readable; image-only chart defaults were not transcribed.

| Stage | Activities and resulting artifacts |
|---|---|
| Setup | Assemble at most five people including a Decider; reserve two days and a shared workspace. |
| Basics | Select customer and consequential problem; identify capability, insight, and motivation; list products, workarounds, inaction, and toughest competitor. Output: explicit starting assumptions. |
| Differentiation | Assess conventional and custom differentiators; choose two; map competitive positions; write two or three practical principles. Output: differentiation chart and Mini Manifesto. |
| Options | Generate alternative approaches; summarize each with explanation and sketch; Decider selects up to seven to compare. |
| Lenses | Plot options through customer, pragmatic, growth, money, and custom perspectives; review disagreements; choose primary approach and fallback. |
| Hypothesis | Connect customer, problem, approach, competition, and differentiation into one testable statement. |
| Follow-through | Turn its predictions into scorecard questions; test through Design Sprints; adjust and repeat. |

## Design Sprint produces observed customer reactions

This inventory summarizes the authors’ [Design Sprint guide](https://www.character.vc/guide/design-sprint), locators **Set the Stage**, **Monday–Friday**, and **FAQs**.

| Stage | Activities and resulting artifacts |
|---|---|
| Setup | Choose challenge, Decider, facilitator, team of seven or fewer, visiting experts, and five days. |
| Monday | Set goal and risk questions; map customer journey; interview experts; collect, organize, and vote on How Might We notes; choose target customer/moment. |
| Tuesday | Collect Lightning Demos; allocate sketching; produce notes, ideas, Crazy 8s, and solution sketches. Start recruiting screened customers. |
| Wednesday | Review sketches with heat maps, critiques, straw poll, and Decider vote; combine winners or compare competing prototypes; storyboard the test. |
| Thursday | Assign production/interview roles; build and integrate realistic prototype; trial it; prepare interview script and confirm participants. |
| Friday | Test with five target customers; use welcome, context, introduction, tasks, and debrief; observe together; record scorecard evidence, decisions, and next steps. |

The FAQ recommends Foundation Sprint first, cautions that solo work loses team benefits, and allows individual techniques outside full sprints. It recommends five days for a first full sprint.

## Two supporting guides make the conversations operational

**Decision mechanics:** The authors’ [Note and Vote guide](https://www.character.vc/guide/note-and-vote), **How it Works, steps 1–6**, specifies a clear question, independent silent answers, silent sharing, silent voting, optional short debate, and final Decider choice. Votes inform rather than bind the decision. Anonymous contributions and independent thinking are intentional mechanisms. Agent discussion should not erase them.

**Customer research:** Michael Margolis’s [GV research sprint](https://medium.com/gv-library/the-gv-research-sprint-a-4-day-process-for-answering-important-startup-questions-97279b532b25), published 2014-08-04, updated June 2021, **Components of a research sprint** and **The four days**, supplies the linked research backbone. Required inputs include agreed questions, selective recruiting, a realistic prototype, five individual qualitative interviews, and shared real-time synthesis. Its schedule covers screening, scheduling, interview-guide preparation, confirmation, interviews, and findings. It explicitly limits what this research can answer: for example, analytics quantities and database architecture need other methods. Detailed day-specific articles were linked but not reviewed in this baseline.

## Proposed skill adaptations, separate from the sources

These are design recommendations for this user’s room, **not instructions attributed to the authors**:

1. Maintain a coverage ledger per idea: activity, source/locator, required output, existing evidence, gaps, and completion state. Preserve an explicit “source not yet reviewed” state.
2. Credit existing work only after checking purpose, evidence, and scope against the exercise; fill missing pieces. This implements the user’s confirmed preference.
3. Keep the user as Decider. AI mentor perspectives can challenge assumptions and suggest alternatives; they are neither these real people nor independent customer evidence.
4. Adapt timeboxes into resumable conversations while retaining written outputs and deliberate alternatives. Label the result a conversational adaptation rather than a literal two-day/five-day workshop.
5. For an existing feature, establish the affected users, current journey, baseline behavior, and competing ways to solve the problem. Preserve useful component ideas even when the whole concept fails; evaluate one idea at a time, as requested.
6. Require actual research artifacts before marking customer testing complete. Synthetic personas, agent votes, attractive prototypes, and completed worksheets alone cannot establish demand.
7. Make the exit decision evidence-qualified: pursue a bounded next investment, revise/retest, stop, or insufficient evidence. Attach unresolved feasibility, economics, distribution, or other risks for relevant expert sessions. Completing this source inventory is not completing idea validation.

Before a final reusable skill claims all Knapp–Zeratsky recommendations are covered, resolve the board/book gaps and run a coverage review. These notes are sufficient to design and test an initial, clearly scoped version.
