## Selection begins with a hypothesis and its missing evidence

The book asks three questions: which hypothesis type is at issue—desirability, feasibility, or viability; how much evidence exists for that **specific hypothesis**; and how soon a decision is needed. Start cheaply when uncertainty is high, select the strongest feasible evidence under the constraints, use further experiments to examine the same hypothesis, and reduce uncertainty before expensive construction. These are selection principles, not a requirement to march through every technique. Multiple experiments can share participants, recruitment bias, assumptions, or instrumentation; quantity alone does not establish independence. [Printed 92–93 / PDF 108–109](derived/testing-business-ideas/page-108.md), [selection rules](derived/testing-business-ideas/page-109.md).

**Room adaptation:** ask which claim would change the next decision, who can supply relevant evidence, what they must actually do, and what result would support, contradict, or leave the claim unresolved. Specify the idea/component version, audience, context, channel, price, denominator, observation window, threshold rationale, resources, and next action. Reuse an existing artifact only after checking that its participants, conditions, and observations answer this question. An agent-generated persona or simulated customer response supplies no independent customer evidence.

## Source defects and evidence boundaries affect execution

**Classification:** the first five interaction-prototype cards in validation print “DISCOVERY” above their titles while their page margins and section inventory say validation. This was visually confirmed at PDF 252, 256, 260, 264, and 270. Pretend to Own's subtype heading also differs from its inventory grouping; Card Sorting and Buy a Feature have discussion-prototype headings despite their preference/prioritization grouping. Keep the 29/15 classification from the inventories; subtype headings are not a reliable routing schema. [Validation inventory](derived/testing-business-ideas/page-250.md), [Clickable Prototype](derived/testing-business-ideas/page-252.md), [Pretend to Own](derived/testing-business-ideas/page-224.md), [Card Sorting](derived/testing-business-ideas/page-238.md).

**Metrics:** printed Link Tracking and Feature Stub divide exposures by actions while discussing conversion percentages. Partner & Supplier Interviews divides interviews by bids. Email Campaign calls unique clicks divided by unique opens an open rate. Presale calls completed purchases divided by checkout entrants abandonment, and Letter of Intent divides sent documents by signatures. These are source-level problems, visually verified at PDF 130, 170, 174, 180, 292, and 312. Similar exposure/action reversals occur in other cards. [Link details](derived/testing-business-ideas/page-170.md), [feature details](derived/testing-business-ideas/page-174.md), [email details](derived/testing-business-ideas/page-180.md), [presale details](derived/testing-business-ideas/page-292.md), [intent details](derived/testing-business-ideas/page-312.md).

**Room correction:** define each metric from its actual question before collecting data. A conversion fraction normally uses qualifying actions divided by eligible exposures, with compatible cohorts, uniqueness rules, and windows. Checkout abandonment can use entrants minus completers, divided by entrants, only with compatible definitions. Record this as our correction rather than silently attributing corrected formulas to the book. Preserve zero-denominator and tracking failures as unmeasurable, not zero demand.

**Heuristics:** interview counts, ad or signup targets, referral benchmarks, cost/time bands, and survey cutoffs are contextual author guidance. The validation survey's 40% discussion belongs to the Sean Ellis question and recently experienced users; its diagram distinguishes “very” from “somewhat” disappointed, while the later details shorten this to “disappointed.” Do not merge those responses or treat 40% as a universal product-market-fit certificate. NPS asks about hypothetical referrals. Split-test confidence suggestions likewise do not establish a universal sample or stopping rule. [Survey diagram, PDF 296](derived/testing-business-ideas/page-296.md), [NPS, PDF 297](derived/testing-business-ideas/page-297.md), [shortened survey wording](derived/testing-business-ideas/page-298.md), [split-test procedure](derived/testing-business-ideas/page-287.md).

**Commitment and delivery:** preserve the distinctions among clicks, contact details, pretend spending, written intent, payment information, pledges, orders, money collected, repeated use, and fulfillment. Concierge and Wizard of Oz differ in whether manual delivery is visible. Presale, Mock Sale, and Crowdfunding create different observations and obligations. Source descriptions of contractual status or payment-provider practices are not a current legal determination. A 404 Test and Feature Stub deliberately affect a real experience; the book's brief exposure and noncritical-use cautions matter when selecting them. Boomerang explicitly keeps the competitor's branding rather than presenting its product as ours. [Feature Stub scope](derived/testing-business-ideas/page-172.md), [duration and controls](derived/testing-business-ideas/page-174.md), [404 Test](derived/testing-business-ideas/page-176.md), [Boomerang](derived/testing-business-ideas/page-221.md).

## Existing mentor methods can share evidence without becoming interchangeable

- **Savoia:** Pretend to Own explicitly overlaps the Pinocchio approach; Wizard of Oz, Mock Sale, and Pop-Up Store relate to Mechanical Turk, Fake Door, and One-Night Stand techniques. This library also accepts interviews, surveys, and existing data for early discovery. Keep those useful for learning without relabeling them direct behavioral demand evidence under Savoia's stricter standard. [Savoia methods](the-right-it-methods.md), [evidence distinctions](the-right-it-evidence-and-fit.md).
- **Pincus:** start with the smallest informative test. His Minimum Idea State can precede this book's functioning Single Feature MVP; a Mash-Up delivers an actual service, while a Ripomatic may explain a concept. Boomerang's observation of competitor use can inform deconstruction without establishing demand for the recombination. [Pincus idea methods](pincus-idea-methods.md), [iteration and fit](pincus-iteration-and-fit.md).
- **Pattern Breakers:** a poor result can concern audience, offer, implementation, or insight. The library supplies ways to distinguish these; it does not require a category breakthrough or venture-scale ambition. Attach each result to the tested configuration before abandoning a whole idea or transferring confidence to an atomic component. [Pattern Breakers evidence and fit](pattern-breakers-evidence-and-fit.md).
- **Click/Sprint:** the Foundation Sprint's hypothesis and risk work can guide experiment selection; storyboards and interactive prototypes can share artifacts with this library. Matching artifact names do not establish matching preparation, audience, protocol, or question. Preserve the user's mandatory Knapp–Zeratsky coverage, credit existing qualifying work, and count an observed session once even when several methods use it. [Knapp–Zeratsky baseline](knapp-zeratsky-baseline.md).

These cross-method connections are room adaptations, not claims that the authors prescribe a combined sequence. After a substantial session, recommend the next relevant question and expert lens, explain omissions, and keep a separate decision history for each idea and component.
