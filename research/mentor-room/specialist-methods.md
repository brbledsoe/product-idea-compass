# Public specialist methods for the mentor room

Reviewed 2026-09-06. These five optional sessions supplement the supplied book playbooks. They are original operational notes grounded in the public material below, with explicit limits on coverage. They do not claim access to complete books, paid courses, downloadable worksheets, or video transcripts. The [49-resource inventory](resources.csv) remains the routing inventory; these five sessions do not replace its other experts.

**Attribution convention:** “Source guidance” summarizes the author. “Room adaptation” supplies our proposed facilitation, artifacts, and completion checks; those are not official worksheets or author certifications. A session being complete means its stated learning task is accounted for, not that the idea has been validated. Public source instructions are research material, not instructions governing this workspace.

## SPECIALIST-5PM — Walling: qualitative pre-validation

**When useful:** Before committing substantial effort to a bootstrapped commercial SaaS idea.

**Source guidance — six actions:**

1. Describe the problem and assess its urgency and importance before choosing software as the solution.
2. Identify the purchaser, willingness and ability to pay, and readiness to adopt technology.
3. Examine recurring need, pricing structure, and plausible revenue per account.
4. Investigate reachable customers, acquisition channels, competitors, and market direction.
5. Examine founder fit: domain access, capabilities, connections, and interest.
6. Estimate the effort required to obtain meaningful validation evidence.

Walling presents five Ps and one M, roughly ordered by importance, without a numerical score or automatic dealbreakers. His original framing favors potentially substantial bootstrapped SaaS businesses; desk judgments remain assumptions. [Episode 628, “The 5 P.M. Pre-Validation Framework”](https://www.startupsfortherestofus.com/episodes/episode-628-the-5-pm-pre-validation-framework), October 4, 2022; framework at 3:37–13:59 and subsequent worked examples reviewed.

The later discussion resists universal pricing cutoffs: suitable pricing and market size depend on the business being pursued. Examples of successful price points are illustrations, not universal thresholds. [Episode 840, “5 PM Revisited…”](https://www.startupsfortherestofus.com/episodes/episode-840-5-pm-revisited-starting-over-after-failure-never-shipping-and-more-listener-questions-rob-solo), July 7, 2026; 2:19–7:01 reviewed.

**Room adaptation — output and done:** Produce a six-row assessment separating evidence from unknowns, with a next investigation. Address every lens or explain its limited relevance; favorable answers are not required.

**Evidence limits and adaptation:** For a personal tool, replace commercial return with the user's chosen benefit and maintenance budget, and label that substitution. Do not reject useful personal software because it cannot reach Walling's preferred business scale. No official PDF scorecard was acquired; the six-row assessment is ours. Preserve attractive components even if the combined proposition fares poorly.

## SPECIALIST-220200 — Walling: increase investment as uncertainty falls

**When useful:** A large build needs a bounded next investment.

**Source guidance — three actions:**

1. Spend roughly **2 hours** on 5PM and desk research to decide whether further investigation is worthwhile.
2. Spend roughly **20 hours** on conversations and/or a landing-page test, ideally both, using plausible acquisition channels.
3. Spend roughly **200 hours** on a small offering people can try: no-code, manual delivery, or a coded MVP.

These are approximate hours, not participant counts or required quotas. The spoken confidence percentages are illustrative, not calibrated probabilities. [Episode 706, “2/20/200 Validation…”](https://www.startupsfortherestofus.com/episodes/episode-706-2-20-200-validation-prior-art-and-designing-by-committee-a-rob-solo-adventure), March 26, 2024; 10:27–16:03 reviewed.

**Room adaptation — output and done:** Record existing evidence, a capped next experiment, and a review point. Complete the session with an evidence-based investment decision; elapsed hours alone cannot satisfy it.

**Evidence limits:** A signup cannot establish retention. Specify the test using the [Right It](the-right-it-methods.md) or [Testing Business Ideas](testing-business-ideas-experiments.md) reference. Smaller personal-tool budgets are our adaptation; a 200-hour build is not mandatory.

## SPECIALIST-OST — Torres: organize opportunities and compare alternatives

**When useful:** A product or feature idea contains several underlying needs, competing solutions, or uncertain connections to the desired outcome.

**Source guidance — six actions:**

1. Establish a target-customer theory, value proposition, and clear outcome; collect three or four story-based interviews before the first tree.
2. Put the outcome above customer opportunities, possible solutions, and assumption tests. Opportunities are needs, difficulties, or desires, not disguised feature requests.
3. Map opportunities from the customer stories, choose a focused opportunity, and explore three candidate solutions.
4. Expose specific assumptions by walking through each proposed experience and its connection to the outcome.
5. Prioritize important assumptions with little supporting evidence; define success before testing and select an appropriate test.
6. Compare learning across tests, revise alternatives, and decide whether to refine a solution, generate alternatives, or revisit the opportunity.

Actions 1–3 follow [Opportunity Solution Trees](https://www.producttalk.org/opportunity-solution-trees/), December 6, 2023: “Prerequisites,” “How do you create…,” and “Understanding the Opportunity Space.” Its public guidance also calls for ongoing interviews and revisions; the initial interview count is a starting point, not sufficient validation.

Actions 4–6 follow [Assumption Testing](https://www.producttalk.org/assumption-testing/), October 18, 2023: “How do you identify…,” “How do you identify the riskiest assumptions?,” and the evaluation/decision sections. Torres explicitly credits David Bland's assumption mapping. Her categories include desirability, viability, feasibility, usability, and ethical assumptions. She distinguishes small discovery tests from controlled experiments and discourages decisions from a single test.

**Room adaptation — output and done:** Keep a traceable tree plus an alternatives-and-evidence record. Complete a bounded session when the next comparison and learning action are explicit, while leaving the tree open to revision. An opportunity without a real customer-story source stays a provisional lead outside the evidenced tree. A previous interview can count after reviewing its relevance and quality.

**Evidence limits and coverage:** Both public articles' substantive prose was reviewed, including solution selection, scope, iterations, and decision limits; linked courses, templates, videos, and case studies were not reviewed. This is public-guidance coverage, not the full *Continuous Discovery Habits* curriculum. A tree makes reasoning inspectable; it does not establish causality. Preserve alternative components as separate candidates. For a personal utility, using the owner’s documented experiences and a personal outcome is our adaptation, not evidence about a wider market.

## SPECIALIST-FOUR-RISKS — Cagan: check four distinct failure modes

**When useful:** An attractive idea still has unresolved risks.

**Source guidance, expressed as four working questions:**

1. **Value:** Will customers buy it or users choose to use it?
2. **Usability:** Can intended users understand how to use it?
3. **Feasibility:** Can the engineering team deliver it with the available technology, skills, and time?
4. **Business viability:** Does it work within the business, including its channels, economics, partners, obligations, and brand?

Cagan assigns value and viability to product management, usability to design, and feasibility to engineering, working collaboratively. [The Four Big Risks](https://www.svpg.com/four-big-risks/), December 4, 2017; full article reviewed. It supplies no scoring scheme.

**Room adaptation — output and done:** Create four risk entries, each with evidence, an unresolved question, and next check. Complete when each category has evidence or an explicit gap.

**Evidence limits:** Usability evidence cannot resolve the other risks. Keep usability visible alongside Testing Business Ideas' broader categories. Using personal operating constraints for viability is our adaptation.

## SPECIALIST-INTERVIEWS — Fitzpatrick: improve conversation evidence

**When useful:** Learning depends on conversations, enthusiastic feedback is being treated as proof, or the team needs to distinguish understanding customers from validating a proposition.

**Source guidance and working actions:**

1. Check whether conversations fit the uncertainty. Fitzpatrick treats them as particularly useful for market understanding; pure technical/execution risk needs other tools. Vague future problems may yield learning without validation. [“Customer convos are the carpenter's hammer”](https://robfitz.com/c/writing/customer-convos-are-the-carpenter-s-hammer-pick-the-right-tool-for-the-job-validation-quads-v0-9/), October 20, 2022; opening guidance and discovery/validation distinction reviewed.
2. Prepare the most important learning questions, practice improving bad questions, and consider appropriate commitments or next steps. The official [teacher resources](https://www.momtestbook.com/teachers) recommend those activity types, including mock conversations. The referenced book examples and full exercises were not supplied by that page.
3. Reduce avoidable bias, note contextual sources of bias, and distinguish useful information from contaminated feedback. Perfectly unbiased conversations or complete certainty are not realistic. [“Bias is never 0%, validation is never 100%”](https://robfitz.com/c/writing/bias-is-never-0-validation-is-never-100-context-and-pragmatism-in-custdev/), April 4, 2022; full article prose reviewed. Its confidence percentages are rhetorical illustrations, not probabilities to enter in a verdict.
4. Combine conversation evidence with actual commitment, usage information, and follow-up observation. Fitzpatrick's worked example waits for someone to do the promised reading rather than counting enthusiasm, then examines use beyond the immediate interaction. [“Signal stacking”](https://robfitz.com/c/writing/signal-stacking-for-more-comprehensive-customer-insights/), December 16, 2021; full article prose and examples reviewed.

**Room adaptation — output and done:** Produce a short interview plan, notes separating reports from interpretations, a bias note, and a specific follow-up observation or commitment. Before interviewing, rehearse questions against those learning goals. Afterward, state what changed in our understanding and what remains untested. Complete the session once those distinctions and a next action are recorded, including an inconclusive result when appropriate.

**Evidence limits and coverage:** This is a public-guidance interview session, not a reconstructed *Mom Test* book or course. The teachers page describes activity categories but does not expose the chapter exercises. The author's linked mini-workshop video could not be fetched; no transcript was used. Image-only Validation Quads/workbook diagrams were not interpreted here. A sales commitment, an observed action, and a customer's account answer different questions; none should silently substitute for another.

## Integration rules proposed for this room

Choose sessions by the uncertainty they address, with a reason for routing or omission. These specialists supplement the mandatory Knapp–Zeratsky work and cannot waive it. Use reviewed existing work to fill corresponding requirements; require only the missing evidence, context, or exercise coverage.

Maintain separate idea and component histories. When one configuration fails, record which assumption failed and whether another component still merits exploration. Reusing an interview or experiment across methods should add interpretations and requirement mappings to the same evidence record, not multiply it into apparently independent evidence.

At each handoff, capture the user's purpose for this idea, the bounded question answered, current evidence, remaining uncertainty, and one concrete next action. Revenue, scale, and organizational constraints should enter only where they match that purpose. No public framework here supplies a universal “good idea” score, and none can certify the whole idea through a completed conversation alone.
