## Question

Should every expert receive a session or be considered for relevance; when can existing work satisfy mandatory Knapp–Zeratsky activities; and should each idea define its own ambition and success criteria? Resolve through Brandon's responses. The mandatory core's early-stop boundary belongs to the subsequent coverage-contract decision.

## Comments

2026-09-05 — Brandon chose to consider every expert, recommend relevant sessions, and explain omissions. He also confirmed that existing work should count when checked against the method, with gaps filled.

2026-09-05 — On idea-specific success criteria, Brandon answered yes with a clarification: he brings one idea at a time, each with a separate and unique session. An idea may contain smaller or atomic ideas; a component may have promise even when the current combination or context does not.

## Answer

Confirmed through Brandon's live responses:

- Consider every expert in the inventory; recommend relevant sessions and explain omissions.
- Credit existing work only after checking it against the applicable method, then fill the gaps.
- Establish success criteria for each idea rather than impose one business ambition on every idea.
- Keep each idea's exploration separate. Preserve its component ideas so a negative judgment on the whole does not erase a potentially useful part.

These are requirements, not claims that a particular routing, evidence model, or session design has been tested. How component evidence transfers to a new context remains an open decision in the follow-on ticket.
