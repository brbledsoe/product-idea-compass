## Question

Would the mentor room work better if its coordinator invoked an individual skill for each recurring deliverable or sub-outcome, such as forming useful user profiles or conducting competitor research, instead of relying only on supporting playbooks?

## Parked hypothesis from Brandon

2026-09-06 — Brandon suspects that deliverable-focused subskills may be the right architecture, similar to Wayfinder's use of supporting skills. His examples are “help me form good user profiles for my idea” and “help me do good competitor research.” Preserve the idea of a skill per meaningful outcome, not merely one persona or skill per expert.

He wants to test the current room in a new chat first and revisit this if experience supports it. This is a hypothesis to remember, not an instruction to implement the split now or a settled requirement that every artifact must have its own skill.

## When to revisit

Use the real pilot's concrete examples: repeated questions without progress, weak or inconsistent deliverables, unclear completion checks, or recurring procedures that would benefit from focused inputs, instructions, outputs, and handoffs. Compare a targeted instruction/playbook improvement with a genuine subskill boundary. Preserve source attribution, shared evidence identity, and the coordinating room's case history whichever approach is chosen.

The pilot should establish what needs improvement; do not redesign the architecture merely to make it resemble Wayfinder.
