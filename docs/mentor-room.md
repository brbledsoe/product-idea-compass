# Use the mentor room

The first project skill is [mentor-room](../.agents/skills/mentor-room/SKILL.md). It uses the saved [research library](../research/mentor-room/index.md) and creates a separate, resumable case for each idea. [Behavioral trials and structural checks](mentor-room-validation.md) passed after correcting one premature-completion issue; the first real-idea pilot is next.

Start with:

> Use $mentor-room. Here's my idea: …

A rough paragraph is enough. Include existing notes, prototypes, or test results if you have them. The facilitator will help establish what worthwhile means for this idea, identify unknowns and useful components, and suggest the next session. You do not need to organize your idea into a framework beforehand.

To continue later:

> Use $mentor-room to resume my [idea name] case. Here's what happened since last time: …

If the current skill picker has not refreshed, point directly to [its SKILL.md](../.agents/skills/mentor-room/SKILL.md). The skill and its research library currently belong to this project; copying only the skill folder elsewhere would omit its source references.

## What the room does

- One facilitator applies the published methods of the relevant mentors. Knapp–Zeratsky remains the core; the original49 resource entries are considered with reasons for recommended, deferred, or omitted sessions.
- Sessions develop artifacts and experiments through back-and-forth questions. Prior work earns credit after inspection. Each session leaves a concrete next action and a place to resume.
- Cases retain hypotheses, observations, contradictions, components, and decisions. A promising part of a failed combination can be explored separately without inheriting unsupported validation.
- Recommendations distinguish pursuing the next experiment, revising, pausing, stopping, and insufficient evidence. Completing worksheets is separate from demonstrating customer behavior.
- You can stop at any time. Unfinished mandatory work remains labeled **stopped—core incomplete**.

## Current source boundary

The first core has 33 tracked activities spanning the reviewed Click procedures, official guides, and saved combined114-page board. It accounts for the recommended repeat-sprint programme and records conversational/solo adaptations. The full *Sprint* book, separate Click bonus pack, and missing transcripts are not claimed as reviewed.

Operational playbooks cover Knapp–Zeratsky, Pincus, Maples–Ziebelman, Savoia, and Bland–Osterwalder, with all 44 Testing Business Ideas experiments indexed. Five supplementary public-method sessions cover Walling's two frameworks, Torres, Cagan, and Fitzpatrick. Some other catalog specialists still require focused source research before detailed facilitation; the skill records that limitation and can do that research when relevant.

The next useful trial is one real idea. Synthetic trials assess how the facilitator behaves; only actual work and observations can establish evidence about your idea.
