# Knapp–Zeratsky mandatory core

The executable coverage contract is [core.json](core.json). Its stable activity IDs, prompts, outputs, and completion checks are the checklist for each case. Do not replace it with a loose “did a sprint” flag. The contract is a **conversational adaptation**, not a claim that the authors created or endorsed this room.

The current version covers the reviewed procedural Click material, official public Foundation/Design Sprint guides, and saved 114-page combined Character board export. It does **not** establish complete review of the full *Sprint* book, Click bonus pack, podcast transcripts, unframed live canvas, or the partially inspected third-party Mural template. Additional sources may extend a later baseline without rewriting old cases as if they had completed it.

Where source variants differ, this first version chooses:

| Choice | Default and retained alternative |
|---|---|
| Programme | Click's preferred Foundation plus three Design Sprints across four weeks. Keep cycle-level hypothesis, observation and decision history; reuse unchanged artifacts after review. Timing is adaptable; fewer cycles require an explicit adaptation and rationale or incomplete status on stop. Three cycles never prove success. |

Timing and meeting format may change to fit resumable conversations. Preserve activities and artifacts. Setup, Note-and-Vote, expert input, and customer testing require explicit solo/AI adaptations where relevant; an agent cannot manufacture independent people.

## Foundation conversations

Use the core prompts as opening questions, then follow the user's actual uncertainty. Preserve both rough hypotheses and observed facts. A small sequence often works well:

1. Establish the people, consequential problem, founding advantages, and alternatives in a **Basics** artifact. Ask about recent concrete episodes and existing workarounds. Draft the sheet with uncertain claims marked rather than demanding polished answers first.
2. Explore differentiation through independent candidate scales, honest competitor placement, and a reality check. Ask what customers would perceive and whether the team could deliver it. Use Values and practical principles to make a **Mini Manifesto**. A pleasing2x2 is not evidence of an uncontested market.
3. Generate meaningfully different approaches, summarize and sketch each, and expose tradeoffs through classic/custom lenses. Choose a top bet and backup with the human Decider. The growth/money lenses remain considered even for a personal tool; label adaptations to reach, benefit, effort, and maintenance.
4. Assemble the **Founding Hypothesis** and scorecard questions. Check whether customers, problem, approach, differentiation, switching, and the overall proposition need separate observations. Finish with the next important uncertainty, not a declaration that the idea is good.

Read the precise source activity when facilitating it, including chart axes and source variants. The main contract's summaries are retrieval aids, not a substitute for unclear source mechanics. Shared artifacts may satisfy several rows after checking the required content.

## Design Sprint conversations and fieldwork

Start from the current foundation and chosen learning target. Set goal/metrics, identify risks, gather actual expertise and organize How Might We opportunities, then map actors, discovery, core experience, and outcome. Record unavailable expertise as a gap; an AI synthesis can be useful but is not an interview with the named expert.

Collect relevant demonstrations, sketch independently through notes/ideas/Crazy8s/solution sketches, review candidates, and let the Decider choose what to prototype. Then write the two mini hypotheses explaining why the selected solutions could address goals, metrics, and risks. Decide whether to combine solutions or compare them; produce a storyboard for the actual study flow. Begin recruiting while preparing the prototype, with named ownership and screening.

Build only the realistic experience needed for the learning target, assign roles, rehearse the flow, and prepare a neutral interview guide. Include discovery/offer framing when that is a critical risk. Use welcome, context, prototype introduction, tasks, and debrief; let participants attempt the experience without coaching them to success.

Customer testing stays pending until actual screened participants have been observed. Save observation records and source context before synthesizing patterns. Separate what people did, what they reported, observer interpretations, and Decider conclusions. A five-person qualitative study may reveal design and value issues; it does not estimate population conversion, prove retention, or validate automation/economics.

Review the scorecard and hot takes, retain contradictions, then assign next steps. Keep a cycle record for each sprint, linking the hypothesis version, reused and changed artifacts, actual observations, and decision. Account for the recommended three cycles in `KZ-REPEAT`; fewer cycles are a recorded adaptation or an incomplete stop, not silent standard completion. Prior plans/maps/sketches may be reused after checking the changed scope; don't rerun irrelevant work solely to imitate a calendar.

## What completion means

A row is done or credited only when its output is inspected and its completion check is satisfied. Conditional follow-through rows may be considered with a reasoned applicability decision. An exercise discussed but not performed stays pending. Ordinary session next actions belong in the case handoff; they do not complete `DS-NEXT-STEPS`, which requires actual customer-study observations and synthesis. Preparing a scorecard likewise does not complete `DS-SYNTHESIS`.

The case checker reports `incomplete`, `complete`, `complete-adapted`, or `stopped—core incomplete`. `complete` refers only to this pinned source contract; `complete-adapted` exposes departures such as solo decision mechanics or a documented interview-count variant. The facilitator must still review the artifacts and evidence; the checker cannot determine their truth or methodological adequacy.

An interim recommendation can fund the next experiment while the core remains incomplete. A final summary must state the actual core status, source scope, and investment evidence separately. The user may stop with unfinished work and retain useful components; do not coerce continued exercises or relabel that case complete.
