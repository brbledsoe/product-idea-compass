# Experiment sessions

These playbooks turn published practices into conversational sessions. Read source notes for exact locators before selecting a technique. An experiment can serve several methods while remaining one observation stream.

## The Right It

Read [hypotheses and techniques](../../../../research/mentor-room/the-right-it-methods.md), then [evidence and decisions](../../../../research/mentor-room/the-right-it-evidence-and-fit.md) when interpreting results.

**Use for:** an important demand or usage claim that can be tested before substantial construction. Prerequisite: provisional audience and next investment. Clarify these before selecting a test.

1. Name the engagement: investigate, try, buy, return, or recommend. Ask exactly who is eligible, what they will do, at what price/effort, and within what period. Write the Market Engagement Hypothesis, then XYZ with a meaningful denominator and a reason for the proposed threshold (`RIGHTIT-MEH`, `RIGHTIT-XYZ`).
2. Hypozoom to a reachable sample while recording the bridge to the broader claim. Changing purchases into email signups changes the claim; it does not merely shrink the test. A one-person personal-tool test can measure actual use and benefit without pretending to estimate a market percentage (`RIGHTIT-HYPOZOOM`).
3. Compare two or three applicable techniques. Choose by the uncertainty, audience access, realistic commitment, and resources. Consult the source before implementation:

| Technique | Useful observation | Still unproved |
|---|---|---|
| Mechanical Turk / manually delivered facade | Engagement with a delivered experience | Automation, scalability, sustainable costs |
| Pinocchio stand-in in actual routines | Use occasions and interaction fit | Broad demand beyond observed users |
| Fake door | Exposed audience's attempted engagement | Fulfilled orders, revenue, retention |
| Video / live demo with a commitment | Response to the represented proposition | Actual product performance or repeat value |
| Temporary trial / One-Night Stand | Actual engagement in a limited setting | Everyday demand in other contexts |
| Authorized Infiltrator / Relabel | Choice in a particular distribution setting | Transfer to a different channel or actual new performance |
| Morsel / tiny usable sample | Consumption and commitment for more | Full-product retention and economics |

4. Complete the shared experiment contract before collecting results: hypothesis/version, recruitment, denominator, measured behavior, thresholds and rationale, duration, budget, delivery/disclosure, and stop condition. Prepare the artifact and participant instructions. Record planned versus executed work accurately.
5. Read actual counts and observations, separating acquisition from repetition and intention from fulfillment. Save alternative explanations and whether the experiment answered its own question. Decide the next bounded investment or a discriminating follow-up.

**Done:** an executable experiment with a measurement/decision contract; after running, raw observations and a scoped conclusion. Savoia's 10/30/50/70/90% Right It Index and relative Caliper points are heuristics, not calibrated likelihoods. Three-to-five experiments is not a participant minimum. BusU is a fictional teaching case. Buy-intent clicks are not customers, and pickup is not purchase. External research can inform a hypothesis; it cannot supply this idea's customer behavior.

## Testing Business Ideas

Read [process and cards](../../../../research/mentor-room/testing-business-ideas-process.md). For technique selection, use the [44-experiment index](../../../../research/mentor-room/testing-business-ideas-experiment-index.json) and [experiment notes](../../../../research/mentor-room/testing-business-ideas-experiments.md). The index preserves 29 discovery and 15 validation entries, exact PDF page spans, and technique-specific limits. Load selected source pages instead of all 365 pages.

**Use for:** a tangled assumption set, choosing the next experiment, conflicting evidence, or deciding whether to persevere/pivot/stop. Prerequisite: the current proposition and next investment; a rough business/value map is sufficient.

1. Expose desirability, feasibility, and viability assumptions; distinguish usability when helpful. Business/value canvases are optional representations, useful only if they reveal missing logic. Ask what must be true and which single failure would undermine the next investment. Split bundled claims (`TBI-HYPOTHESIS`, `TBI-RISK-TYPES`).
2. Place importance against evidence, challenging the evidence already claimed. In the source map, Important is up, Have Evidence is left, No Evidence is right; the important/no-evidence quadrant is the priority. Output: a ranked uncertainty set with supporting/contradicting evidence IDs (`TBI-ASSUMPTIONS-MAP`).
3. Select an experiment from the indexed library for the exact claim, stage, audience, and resources. Explain why the selected technique can observe the behavior and why plausible alternatives fit less well. Choose by learning value, not a memorized cheapest-test ladder (`TBI-EXP-*`).
4. Draft a Test Card: Hypothesis, Test, Metric, Criteria, with owner/deadline/duration. Add the case experiment fields for recruitment, context, threshold rationale, and limits. Use the original PDF if visual card fields matter; text extraction misses parts of the cards (`TBI-TEST-CARD`).
5. After running, create a Learning Card: Hypothesis, Observation, Learnings/Insights, Decisions/Actions. Keep customer need and delivery economics distinct; a concierge test may support one while undermining the other (`TBI-LEARNING-CARD`).
6. Review evidence quality for this claim: provenance, participant selection, statements versus actions, setting, commitment, repetition, and contradictory outcomes. Choose stronger/different evidence when repeated weak tests do not resolve uncertainty. Update the experiment board Backlog → Setup → Run → Learn (`TBI-EVIDENCE-STRENGTH`, `TBI-CONFIDENCE`, `TBI-EXPERIMENT-FLOW`).
7. Recommend further investment, revision, stop, or more learning. A pivot triggers an applicability review of prior evidence (`TBI-DECIDE`). Scale ceremonies, committees, and portfolio funding to actual organizational needs; they are not mandatory for a solo idea case.

**Done:** a prioritized claim, a suitable experiment contract, and—only once run—traceable observations, interpretation, decision, and next action. The authors' confidence labels, pictogram ratings, and example thresholds are not statistical probabilities. Check the experiment notes for known source ratio-label errors rather than copying formulas blindly. The 40% disappointment benchmark requires experienced users and remains a contextual diagnostic, not a raw-idea PMF certificate.
