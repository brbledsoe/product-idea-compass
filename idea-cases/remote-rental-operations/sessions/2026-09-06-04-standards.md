# Session 4 — standards, trust, and extra-work exceptions

Question: What do the family inspections contribute?

Method: continued Knapp–Zeratsky Foundation problem clarification. New information changes the strength and interpretation of the readiness hypothesis; no new specialist or technical implementation is needed.

## Reported observations

Brandon clarified that the family usually found the house fairly acceptable, with no major cleaning failure reported. Early repeat visits were intended to teach the primary cleaner the family's property-specific standards, give feedback about acceptable/unacceptable work, and correct shortfalls before arrival. Mom's desired standard was described as comparable to a reasonably nice hotel; it is a founder-defined expectation, not an independently audited standard.

Examples included dust in the cabinet containing the router, insufficient towels, unrefilled coffee, failure to report depleted cleaning supplies, and a missing chocolate on a bed. These are not equivalent in consequence. Supplies needed to perform work, guest provisions, visible cleanliness and presentation details should retain distinct meanings. Some tasks may require supplies or explicit scope agreement rather than more inspection.

The family did some correction work as well as checking and coaching. Actual current inspection frequency, whether it has declined as the cleaner learned, and the criteria for ending repeat visits are still unknown. A recurring commercial demand cannot be inferred from an initial provider-learning period alone.

## Hypothetical exception, not a historical incident

The muddy-boots/backyard-party example was introduced as a what-if. It has not been counted as an observed guest violation, extra cleaning event, damage event, or payment dispute. Brandon proposes allowing the cleaner to show exceptional conditions and seek agreement about extra time and fair additional compensation. The cleaner currently has a fixed price, according to Brandon. Photo fabrication is not the intended current focus.

Candidate workflow: cleaner reports the unusual condition; owner and cleaner agree any changed scope, price and completion time; outcome is recorded; factual context can inform a separately reviewed guest-review draft. This is a design hypothesis. Extra effort alone does not determine guest responsibility or authorize automatic penalties/reviews. No policy claim, payment action, additional staffing or implementation occurred.

## Updated interpretation

Evidence now supports occasional minor gaps plus coaching, correction and confidence-building in this family. It weakens an interpretation that regular visits mainly respond to widespread serious cleaning failures. The question is whether the useful product is an initial standards/onboarding workflow, an ongoing readiness workflow, or a combination with exceptional-work coordination. No choice is settled.

Existing H14 retains its original claim and now carries a mixed assessment. H15 remains untested: software may reduce some visits, but the current steady-state burden must be established before choosing an experiment. The broader urgent incident-response problem is retained.
