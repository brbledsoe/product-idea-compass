## Two distinct jobs

1. **Confidence before arrival:** someone already visiting for cleaning should leave the house ready and provide enough trustworthy information that family members can avoid a second visit.
2. **Recovery during a stay:** someone must turn a guest's report into a diagnosis/next action, coordinate suitable help and access, and confirm resolution without repeated family calls.

The second is described as the most stressful; the first is the most frequent regular work described. Frequency alone does not establish which consumes the most total hours or has the strongest commercial opportunity.

## Proposed first learning focus

Investigate what the family inspection contributes. It is a recurring, accessible workflow that can be studied while the current family checks continue. No guests need to be exposed to an untested readiness claim.

Candidate outcome: reduce avoidable second visits while maintaining agreed readiness standards and keeping provider effort acceptable. This does not promise a perfect house or choose sensors, cameras, AI, more checkboxes, or a provider marketplace as the answer.

Keep these explanations open:

- The family catches specific omissions because standards, assigned tasks, time or supplies are insufficient.
- Work is usually complete but the handover does not supply the evidence needed to trust it.
- The visit includes tasks outside the cleaner's scope, such as testing equipment, restocking, putting out instructions or resolving faults.
- Findings vary, making a combination of explicit responsibilities, concise evidence and exception handling more useful than one sensor or image check.

These are facilitator hypotheses. The user's current preference to avoid invasive monitoring is preserved. Hardware remains a possible targeted tool once an observable condition is identified; no sensor specification or monitoring installation is proposed.

The earlier substitute-cleaner handoff idea remains available but is secondary until inspection findings show where the current workflow fails. Existing competitor overlap remains relevant. The broader incident-response need is retained even if the first test addresses readiness.
