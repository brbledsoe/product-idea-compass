# Readiness technology: useful evidence and remaining uncertainty

2026-09-06. Artifact A08. Research appendix supporting the remote-rental-operations case. Sources accessed 2026-09-06. This is a bounded review of official documentation, not a product trial, hardware audit, or exhaustive technology survey.

## Specific checks are feasible; whole-house certification is unproven

Brandon relayed his mother's recurring walkthrough findings: displaced furniture and lamps, coffee presentation, towel quantities, wrinkled bedding, paper-towel fragments, entrance smudges, and cooling before arrival. Her observations are participant reports, not independently inspected defects. They combine presentation, stock, cleanliness, and equipment readiness; each needs different evidence.

The reviewed sources support collecting targeted evidence and reviewing exceptions. They do not establish that one sensor, laser, image model, or combined system can certify the whole property to her standards. That is a limitation of this evidence, not a claim that no other technology exists.

| Mother's check | Candidate evidence to test | What it cannot establish alone |
|---|---|---|
| Chair below picture; couch and lamp placement; inviting coffee setup | Current, well-lit images from the same angle as an approved reference | Objects outside the frame; whether every item is clean or functional |
| Toilet seat/paper presentation; visible bed wrinkles | Close enough completion images with explicit acceptable examples | Hidden dirt, smells, whether linen was changed, or later disturbance |
| Shower debris, paper fragments, entrance smudges | Targeted close-ups plus provider exception reporting | Small defects obscured by lighting, resolution, reflections, or framing |
| Sufficient towels, coffee and cleaning supplies | Visible counts against a stated quantity; targeted stock report | Contents of closed storage or consumption after capture |
| House cool before arrival | Fresh measured temperature, target setting, device connectivity, and provider check | Comfort in every room or continued operation after the reading |

These mappings are proposed test designs, not demonstrated detection performance. A useful readiness report would identify what was checked, when, what remained unknown, and who handles an exception.

## Existing products overlap with this direction

**Properly documents reference-photo verification.** It instructs providers to use matching vantage points and good lighting, and says verification photos must be captured live rather than selected from a gallery. That supplies a concrete comparator for furniture, amenities, and bed-presentation checks. Live capture reduces one reuse pathway; it is not proof of every condition or a fraud guarantee. [Verification-photo instructions](https://help.getproperly.com/en/articles/5470548-take-verification-photos)

**Properly also markets AI-assisted inspection with human review.** Its service describes AI flagging staging, quality, and damage issues in cleaner photos, expert reviewers checking flags/images, provider feedback, and a report before arrival. This is substantial competitive overlap with remote readiness assurance. The published claims do not independently establish accuracy, missed-defect rates, cleaner effort, or how many family visits this household could avoid. Do not treat its advertised error-reduction or revenue figures as proven outcomes. [Real-Time Inspection](https://getproperly.com/real-time-inspection)

**Hospitable markets connected-device oversight.** Its dashboard advertises lock battery/connectivity status, entry logs, current thermostat temperatures, and temperature control for supported devices. These could inform access and cooling checks. The family's device models, compatibility, configuration, and actual reporting reliability remain unknown. A fresh measurement is stronger evidence than a commanded setting; missing or stale telemetry should preserve uncertainty. [Smart-device capabilities](https://hospitable.com/features/smart-devices-management)

**Particle sensing measures a different thing.** EPA explains that low-cost monitors detect specified airborne pollutants or environmental factors and cannot completely represent indoor air quality. Placement and conditions affect results. Inferring clean surfaces, sufficient towels, or correct staging from airborne-particle measurements would exceed that measurement's scope. [EPA capabilities and limitations](https://www.epa.gov/indoor-air-quality-iaq/low-cost-air-pollution-monitors-and-indoor-air-quality)

## Two relevant Airbnb constraints

Airbnb prohibits devices monitoring indoor spaces with cameras/recording even when turned off. Do not assume activation only between stays makes an installed indoor-monitoring proposal acceptable. [Device policy](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/3061)

Airbnb also prohibits asking guests to complete a survey about an Airbnb stay on a non-Airbnb website unless the host is an approved hotel partner. The proposed private iPad webpage form therefore needs revision; optional participation does not create an exception in that wording. [Off-platform feedback policy](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/2799)

The next evidence should compare selected checks with the mother's actual walkthrough findings and measure provider effort, without interpreting a completed checklist or vendor claim as proof of readiness.
