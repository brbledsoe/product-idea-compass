## What changed

Brandon relayed additional feedback from his mother while speaking with her. This is richer evidence from the same family/property, not a separate market interview. Walkthroughs continue before almost every arrival. The earlier possibility that they mainly represent temporary cleaner onboarding is no longer an adequate explanation.

Her checks combine cleanliness, presentation, supplies and functioning equipment: shower debris, paper-towel fragments, door smudges, toilet presentation, furniture and lamp placement, coffee/spoon arrangement, towels, smooth beds and cooling. These are real tasks, even when no major cleaning failure occurs. Whether every task belongs in the cleaner's agreed price remains unknown.

She identifies cleaner acknowledgment/chasing as a major recurring time burden and repair coordination while away as especially overwhelming. Tuesday trash collection also creates visits whenever no one else owns taking the bins to the road. Financial uncertainty is a substantial additional problem: the family cannot readily tell whether the property justifies its spending and work.

An initial customer description is **a remote owner or family coordinator who wants dependable delegation and struggles with fragmented tools**. Mom is a concrete example in her late sixties. This does not establish that age defines the market or that all older owners share her behavior.

The founder's proposed promise can be expressed as: “I can see what is covered, what needs a decision and whether the property is worth the work, without coordinating every person myself.” This is a hypothesis, not a proven offer or a commitment to build all three jobs together.

## The operational distinction

A notice that a cleaning exists does not establish that someone accepted it. Acceptance does not establish completion. A completed checklist does not establish that every readiness standard was met. A repair visit does not establish that the issue was resolved.

A useful candidate handoff records: the person responsible, an agreed deadline, explicit acceptance, completion or an exception, the next action and its owner. For repairs, closure also needs the outcome and any return visit/part still required; the guest should not become the default messenger between contractor and owner. Status must remain unknown when confirmation is missing.

Software can reduce message routing and prompt updates. Availability, physical work and authority to act still require people and agreements. A single interface may be valuable to Mom, but it cannot supply those missing arrangements by itself.

## Proposed X03: two ordinary turnovers

Uses Bland–Osterwalder's Test Card structure from the [saved process note](../../../research/mentor-room/testing-business-ideas-process.md), with a facilitator-designed operational workflow observation. This is not a completed Design Sprint, market validation or a test of fully autonomous software.

- **Hypothesis H18:** explicit acceptance, completion/exception reporting and an agreed backup owner reduce Mom's confirmation chasing without leaving work unowned.
- **Participants:** existing cleaner and family, only after they agree on roles. No messages sent and no live workflow changed.
- **Window:** the next two eligible ordinary turnovers after setup. Dates and practical acceptance/completion cutoffs remain to be agreed.
- **Setup:** choose a responsible person for an unconfirmed or unfinished job; agree the cleaner's task scope and simple reporting channel. Use the existing app or a simple record before building more software. Keep the current walkthroughs and ordinary fallback arrangements during this test.
- **Measures:** timely accepted jobs / 2; jobs with a recorded completion or owned exception by the agreed cutoff / 2; Mom's chasing messages/calls and minutes per job; cleaner reporting minutes; all other family/founder coordination minutes. Record comparable recent work if available, labeling recall as estimated.
- **Proposed success rule:** both jobs explicitly accepted and accounted for by their cutoffs, zero confirmation-chasing by Mom, and no unowned exception. Any reduction must show whose effort replaced hers. Cleaner-burden and total-coordination budgets need participant agreement before the test can be finalized.
- **Failure:** an unowned or missed job, Mom still having to chase confirmation, or a pre-agreed effort budget exceeded. An unresolved operational issue receives the normal human response immediately; observation never takes priority over getting the house ready.
- **Unclear:** fewer than two eligible turnovers, incomplete records, or unsupported effort comparisons. If no exception occurs, the backup route remains untested even if routine coordination succeeds.
- **Why two:** enough for an inexpensive first look at whether the handoff is usable; far too few for a reliability estimate or commercial conclusion. These are proposed local criteria, not published universal thresholds.
- **Next decision:** if useful, compare the workflow against an existing product with an independent host. If it merely moves substantial work onto another relative/founder, revise the automation promise and economics. Test readiness evidence separately against actual walkthrough findings before removing checks.

**One setup question:** For these two turnovers, who besides Mom could own a cleaning job that remains unconfirmed or unfinished? “Nobody yet” identifies a real dependency; it does not justify assuming the founders will provide unpaid ongoing coverage.

## Readiness technology and differentiation

See [A08: technology and its limits](07-readiness-technology.md). Property-specific reference photos, guided live photos, selective review and supported-device telemetry are plausible ingredients. A photograph cannot establish everything Mom checks, and a reassuring report must not hide missing evidence. Rewarding accurate exception reporting is a candidate design principle; rewarding only “perfect” outcomes could discourage reporting, an untested risk to examine with providers.

Properly already advertises AI-assisted photo inspections with human review. That materially challenges an assumed empty market for remote readiness verification. Its published capabilities do not establish accuracy, acceptable cleaner burden or that Mom would stop visiting. Those require a comparison with actual use. ## Financial clarity and operating rules

Airbnb describes reporting gross earnings, deductions, net pay, nights booked and average stay. Those payout figures alone cannot answer the family's question when outside property expenses and unpaid effort remain uncollected. A first finance prototype could reconcile one real month of payouts, recorded costs, exceptional purchases and family hours, mark missing information visibly, and explain the period/basis of each figure. This is a proposed information test, not a calculation of this property's profit or tax treatment. [Airbnb earnings dashboard](https://www.airbnb.com/resources/hosting-homes/a/get-valuable-insights-in-the-earnings-dashboard-675)

Paying every utility through a new app is only one implementation. Manual entries/imports can first test whether the resulting view helps her make a decision. Existing alternatives include Stessa's transaction organization and financial reporting, and PriceLabs' minimum-stay tools. Their presence adds comparison work; it does not prove their suitability for this family's integrations or usability needs. [Stessa](https://www.stessa.com/), [PriceLabs](https://hello.pricelabs.co/blog/minimum-stay-restrictions/)

The eight-hour free late checkout followed by extensive cooking and approximately two extra cleaning hours is a **reported actual event**. Keep it distinct from E28's hypothetical muddy party. It suggests testing an agreed late-checkout rule and an exceptional-work approval/compensation process. A quick cleaning does not by itself establish that a fixed fee is a bad arrangement; scope, quality, availability and predictability also matter. No particular nightly price, minimum stay, surcharge or retroactive guest charge is justified by the current data. Guest age or review recency does not establish likely mess or responsibility for extra charges.

Private feedback, local-event promotion and acquisition alerts remain parked components. The private web-survey idea retains the previously recorded Airbnb policy constraint. None has been selected for the first build.
