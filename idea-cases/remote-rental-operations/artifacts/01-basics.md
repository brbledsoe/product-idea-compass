Session question: **Which host problem is worth investigating before the founders invest in a broader product?**

Method: Knapp–Zeratsky, reviewed Click Basics procedures, PDF pp. 42–66 and checklist pp. 172–174, via [saved method](../../../research/mentor-room/click-book-method.md). This is an initial conversational draft, not a completed sprint or an author endorsement.

## What Brandon reported

The family operates an Airbnb in another country. They purchased connected devices, including door locks, and built an app to automate parts of operating the property. Brandon describes substantial programming and hardware/industrial automation experience in the prospective team. Exact team relationships, ownership, available hours, and the application's working features have not been clarified.

The proposed product is an inexpensive, very easy-to-use app for hosts, especially older hosts. Desired capabilities include cleaning schedules and substitutes, hiring repair providers, grounds maintenance, damage reporting, reviews, promotion through other sites and local event communities, and changing lock access codes. The broader ambition is to provide much of what a management agency does with two programmers and automation.

Brandon has heard that management companies charge substantial fees. No actual management quote or family expense record has been inspected. No third-party host demand, age-related behavior, or willingness to pay has been observed.

## Proposed customer and problem

**Candidate customer:** an owner managing roughly 1–3 short-term rentals from a distance, who wants to retain control and already relies on local people. Property count is a facilitator hypothesis, not a user constraint. Geography, reachable prospects, current software, and willingness to delegate are unknown.

**Candidate problem:** when a local task changes, fails, or remains unconfirmed, the owner must interrupt their day to find someone, establish access and price, follow up, and determine whether the property is ready.

This statement is independent of an app, AI, or a marketplace. It could be wrong: the dominant problem may instead be routine administrative volume, lack of local providers, confusing setup, lack of trustworthy inspections, or weak rental economics.

**Older owners:** retain as a segment to investigate without assuming low technical ability from age. Measure confidence with current tools, accessibility needs, available time, delegation preferences, and actual task completion. The buyer, app operator, and family member configuring the system may be different people.

## Current alternatives

The most useful initial comparisons are Turno, Hospitable, Breezeway, a local co-host/manager, and family coordination through existing calendars/messages/phone calls. Guesty and Evolve provide additional category benchmarks. See the [dated research](02-competitors.md).

**Toughest alternative is unknown.** For the cleaning proposition it may be Turno plus an established cleaner. For a host seeking complete delegation it may be a trusted local person. For a family with rare incidents it may be continuing the current arrangement. Actual customer behavior must decide.

## Critical open premises

1. What country and region contains the property, and where could initial customers be reached?
2. What was the latest actual cleaning/repair intervention? Who did what, how long did it take, what did it cost, and what consequence was avoided?
3. What does the existing application perform successfully today? Which failures still need a person?
4. What should the next investment achieve: family time savings, a first paying customer, or evidence for a business commitment?
5. How often do these incidents occur across a season? What do hosts already spend on software, co-hosting, and their own time?
6. Are dependable primary and backup providers available locally, and who owns unresolved incidents?

The first four topics were asked in three connected questions during this session. Further questions should follow the answers, not become a large questionnaire.

## Working founding hypothesis — not yet selected

If remote small-property owners experience recurring coordination failures despite their current tools, then helping their approved local providers accept, complete, and document work, with clear escalation for unresolved issues, may make them pay for this service over continuing their current arrangement—provided setup and owner interruptions are measurably lower and delivery costs fit the fee.

Separate test questions: Is this the right customer? Is the problem frequent and costly? Does the approach reduce interventions? Is it meaningfully better than the customer's actual alternative? Will someone pay and continue? Can the team operate it within its time and cost constraints?
